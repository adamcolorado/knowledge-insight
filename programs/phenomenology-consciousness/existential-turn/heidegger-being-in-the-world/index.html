Heidegger: Being-in-the-World

Learn Martin Heidegger's account of being-in-the-world from Being and Time: why he turned phenomenology from consciousness to the question of being, how everyday tools reveal a world of practical meaning, and how moods, understanding, and care disclose that world. You'll use the distinction between the ready-to-hand and the present-at-hand to analyze skilled activity in your own life and work. These ideas shaped later accounts of skill, expertise, and embodied cognition, including influential critiques of artificial intelligence.

Module objectives

  • Explain Heidegger's reorientation of phenomenology from consciousness to the question of being
  • Describe Dasein's being-in-the-world through the distinction between readiness-to-hand and presence-at-hand
  • Interpret the roles of mood, understanding, and care in disclosing a world

Further reading

These are the primary texts behind the ideas in this module. Reading them is optional: explore the ones that match your interests. You don't need them to complete the module, which explains their key ideas. Each entry gives the recommended edition, free options where they exist, and where to buy or borrow it.

  • Dreyfus, Hubert L. 1991. Being-in-the-World: A Commentary on Heidegger's Being and Time, Division I. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.
    • Module focus: Chapters 3–6 (worldhood and equipment)
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: MIT Press
    • Free companion: SEP, "Martin Heidegger" (Wrathall)
  • Heidegger, Martin. (1927) 2008. Being and Time. Translated by John Macquarrie and Edward Robinson. Harper Perennial Modern Thought. New York: Harper Perennial.
    • Module focus: Introduction §7; Division I §§12–18, 29, 31, and 41
    • Free: None. Cite by section and German page (H.), which both English translations print in the margin.
    • Publisher: HarperCollins (Harper Perennial Modern Thought; ISBN 97800615755940 · alternative translation: SUNY Press (Stambaugh, rev. Schmidt)
    • Free companion: SEP, "Martin Heidegger" (Wrathall), §2 · IEP, "Heidegger, Martin"
  • Heidegger, Martin. (1979) 1985. History of the Concept of Time: Prolegomena. Translated by Theodore Kisiel. Bloomington: Indiana University Press.
    • Module focus: §§10–13 (Heidegger's critique of Husserl)
    • Free: None; on JSTOR, free through many university libraries.
    • Publisher: Indiana University Press
    • Free companion: SEP, "Martin Heidegger" (Wrathall)
