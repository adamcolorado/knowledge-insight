Course 4 · Module 1

From Phenomenology to the Embodied Mind

Trace how phenomenology moved into cognitive science: Hubert Dreyfus's critique of symbolic AI, the enactive approach of Varela, Thompson, and Rosch, 4E cognition, and neurophenomenology. You'll learn the core claims of each approach, how they draw on Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty, and where they disagree with one another. These frameworks shape current research on AI, perception, and the science of consciousness, so they're essential for reading that literature critically.

Module objectives

  • Explain how Dreyfus used Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty to critique symbolic artificial intelligence
  • Describe the core claims of enactivism and of embodied, embedded, extended, and enactive (4E) cognition
  • Explain neurophenomenology as a method for joining first-person reports with neuroscience

Further reading

These are the primary texts behind the ideas in this module. Reading them is optional: explore the ones that match your interests. You don't need them to complete the module, which explains their key ideas. Each entry gives the recommended edition, free options where they exist, and where to buy or borrow it.

  • Clark, Andy, and David J. Chalmers. 1998. "The Extended Mind." Analysis 58 (1): 7–19.
    • Module focus: The full article
    • Free: Full text on consc.net (Chalmers's site)
  • Dreyfus, Hubert L. 1965. Alchemy and Artificial Intelligence. RAND Paper P-3244. Santa Monica, CA: RAND Corporation.
    • Module focus: The full paper (the critique's first statement)
    • Free: RAND (PDF)
    • Publisher: RAND
  • Dreyfus, Hubert L. 1992. What Computers Still Can't Do: A Critique of Artificial Reason. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.
    • Module focus: Introduction to the 1992 edition
    • Free: None for the book; the original statement of the critique is free: Dreyfus, "Alchemy and Artificial Intelligence" (RAND, 19650
    • Publisher: MIT Press
  • Gallagher, Shaun, and Dan Zahavi. 2021. The Phenomenological Mind. 3rd ed. London: Routledge.
    • Module focus: Chapters 1–2 (methods) and the chapters on the self and on others
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: Routledge
    • Free companion: SEP, "Phenomenological Approaches to Self-Consciousness" (Gallagher and Zahavi)
  • Hutto, Daniel D., and Erik Myin. 2013. Radicalizing Enactivism: Basic Minds without Content. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.
    • Module focus: Chapters 1–2
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: MIT Press
    • Free companion: SEP, "Embodied Cognition" (Spaulding)
  • Lutz, Antoine, and Evan Thompson. 2003. "Neurophenomenology: Integrating Subjective Experience and Brain Dynamics in the Neuroscience of Consciousness." Journal of Consciousness Studies 10 (9–10): 31–52.
    • Module focus: The full article (free)
    • Free: Author-hosted PDF (Thompson's site)
  • Noë, Alva. 2004. Action in Perception. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.
    • Module focus: Chapter 1, "The Enactive Approach to Perception"
    • Free: None for the book; see O'Regan and Noë (2001) for a free statement of the core view.
    • Publisher: MIT Press
  • O'Regan, J. Kevin, and Alva Noë. 2001. "A Sensorimotor Account of Vision and Visual Consciousness." Behavioral and Brain Sciences 24 (5): 939–73.
    • Module focus: The target article (a free statement of the sensorimotor view)
    • Free: Author-posted PDF
    • Publisher: doi:10.1017/S0140525X01000115
  • Thompson, Evan. 2007. Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind. Cambridge, MA: Belknap Press of Harvard University Press.
    • Module focus: Chapters 1–3 (enaction, autopoiesis, sense-making)
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: Harvard University Press
    • Free companion: SEP, "Embodied Cognition" (Spaulding)
  • Varela, Francisco J. 1996. "Neurophenomenology: A Methodological Remedy for the Hard Problem." Journal of Consciousness Studies 3 (4): 330–49.
    • Module focus: The full article
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: Ingenta Connect
  • Varela, Francisco J., Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch. 2016. The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience. Rev. ed. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. First published 1991.
    • Module focus: Chapters 1–2 and 8–10, and the 2016 introductions
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: MIT Press
    • Free companion: SEP, "Embodied Cognition" (Spaulding)
