About this program

Phenomenology began as a demand: go back to the things themselves, and describe experience as it is actually given rather than as our theories say it must be. This program follows that demand from Husserl's founding method through Heidegger's and Merleau-Ponty's existential and embodied revisions, and into the consciousness research that now draws on all three.

You will work through intentionality, the epoche and reduction, time-consciousness, being-in-the-world, the body-subject, and the hard problem, and you will finish each course by writing something of your own: a phenomenological description, an analysis of a skilled activity, an essay on the body-subject, and a position paper on consciousness.

What you will be able to do

  • Describe an experience phenomenologically, using the epoche, eidetic variation, and the noesis-noema structure
  • Trace how Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty reworked Husserl's project and what each change cost and gained
  • Analyze skilled, embodied activity in the vocabulary of being-in-the-world and the body-subject
  • Position yourself in current debates on consciousness, from the hard problem to enactive and predictive accounts

How to progress through a program

Every lesson has the same shape, so you can settle into it and spend your attention on the material.

  1. A motivating readingWhy the question matters, in three minutes.
  2. Four concept readingsThe ideas themselves, one at a time.
  3. A guided close readingOne primary passage, walked through line by line.
  4. A Guided ConversationA structured conversation you run in your own AI account.
  5. An optional journal entryA writing prompt you answer in your own notebook.
  6. A knowledge checkFive questions, ungraded, retry as often as you like.

The last lesson of each module adds a graded quiz with a target score of 80 percent.
