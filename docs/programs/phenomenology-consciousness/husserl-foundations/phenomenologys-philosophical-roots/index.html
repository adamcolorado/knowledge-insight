Phenomenology's Philosophical Roots

Discover the philosophical roots of phenomenology in Descartes, Kant, and Brentano, and why their problems led Husserl to a new method. You'll learn how the method of doubt, the limits of knowledge, and the thesis of intentionality set up phenomenology's central question: how the world shows up in experience. You'll also learn to tell careful description of experience apart from introspection and causal explanation, a distinction that matters in any research that uses first-person reports.

Module objectives

  • Explain how Descartes's method of doubt and Kant's distinction between phenomena and things-in-themselves set the problem phenomenology takes up
  • Describe Brentano's thesis of intentionality and its role as phenomenology's starting point
  • Distinguish phenomenological description from introspective psychology and from causal explanation

Further reading

These are the primary texts behind the ideas in this module. Reading them is optional: explore the ones that match your interests. You don't need them to complete the module, which explains their key ideas. Each entry gives the recommended edition, free options where they exist, and where to buy or borrow it.

  • Brentano, Franz. (1874) 2015. Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint. Translated by Antos C. Rancurello, D. B. Terrell, and Linda L. McAlister. Routledge Classics. London: Routledge.
    • Module focus: Book II, chapter 1
    • Free: No English translation; German original, vol. 1 (1874), Internet Archive
    • Publisher: Routledge
    • Free companion: SEP, "Franz Brentano" (Huemer)
  • Descartes, René. (1641) 2017. Meditations on First Philosophy: With Selections from the Objections and Replies. Translated and edited by John Cottingham. 2nd ed. Cambridge Texts in the History of Philosophy. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
    • Module focus: Meditations I–II
    • Free: Haldane & Ross translation (1911), Wikisource · Bennett's modernized paraphrase, Meditations 1–2 (PDF), Early Modern Texts (paraphrase; don't quote from it)
    • Publisher: Cambridge University Press
  • Kant, Immanuel. (1781/1787) 1998. Critique of Pure Reason. Translated and edited by Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood. The Cambridge Edition of the Works of Immanuel Kant. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.
    • Module focus: B-Preface and Transcendental Aesthetic
    • Free: Kemp Smith translation (1929), Internet Archive (public domain in the US only) · Bennett's paraphrase: Prefaces and Introduction (PDF) and Transcendental Aesthetic (PDF)
    • Publisher: Cambridge University Press
    • Free companion: SEP, "Kant's Views on Space and Time" (Janiak)
