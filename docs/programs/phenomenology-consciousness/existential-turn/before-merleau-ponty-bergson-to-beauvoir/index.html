Before Merleau-Ponty: Bergson to Beauvoir

Meet the thinkers who shaped French phenomenology in the first half of the 20th century: Henri Bergson on lived time, the Gestalt psychologists and Kurt Goldstein on perception and the organism, Jean-Paul Sartre on consciousness and the body, and Simone de Beauvoir on the body as situation. You'll learn how each challenged the idea that experience is built from isolated sensations or moments, and you'll compare competing accounts of how we live our bodies among others. These ideas still inform research on temporal experience, perceptual organization, neuropsychology, and embodiment.

Module objectives

  • Explain Bergson's concept of duration and his critique of spatialized time
  • Describe how Gestalt psychology and Gelb and Goldstein's clinical studies challenged atomistic accounts of perception
  • Compare Sartre's account of consciousness and the body with Beauvoir's account of the body as situation

Further reading

These are the primary texts behind the ideas in this module. Reading them is optional: explore the ones that match your interests. You don't need them to complete the module, which explains their key ideas. Each entry gives the recommended edition, free options where they exist, and where to buy or borrow it.

  • Beauvoir, Simone de. (1949) 2010. The Second Sex. Translated by Constance Borde and Sheila Malovany-Chevallier. New York: Alfred A. Knopf.
    • Module focus: Introduction; Volume I, Part One, chapter 1, "Biological Data"
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: Penguin Random House (Vintage paperback)
    • Free companion: SEP, "Simone de Beauvoir" (Burke), §7
  • Bergson, Henri. (1889) 1913. Time and Free Will: An Essay on the Immediate Data of Consciousness. Translated by F. L. Pogson. London: George Allen.
    • Module focus: Chapter 2, "The Multiplicity of Conscious States: The Idea of Duration"
    • Free: Project Gutenberg 056852 (public domain; ch. 2 is "The Multiplicity of Conscious States: The Idea of Duration")
  • Bergson, Henri. (1896) 1911. Matter and Memory. Translated by Nancy Margaret Paul and W. Scott Palmer. London: Swan Sonnenschein.
    • Module focus: Chapters 1–2 (perception, the body, and the two forms of memory)
    • Free: Internet Archive (public domain)
    • Free companion: SEP, "Henri Bergson" (Moulard-Leonard)
  • Goldstein, Kurt. (1934) 1995. The Organism: A Holistic Approach to Biology Derived from Pathological Data in Man. Foreword by Oliver Sacks. New York: Zone Books.
    • Module focus: The chapters on the Gelb–Goldstein cases and the concrete and abstract attitudes
    • Free: None confirmed; a 1939 English edition is on the Internet Archive, but its rights status is unclear.
    • Publisher: Zone Books
    • Free companion: SEP, "Maurice Merleau-Ponty" (Toadvine) (covers the Schneider case)
  • Köhler, Wolfgang. 1929. Gestalt Psychology. New York: Horace Liveright.
    • Module focus: The chapters on sensory organization
    • Free: Internet Archive (1929 first edition) (public domain in the US; the 1947 revised edition is different and not free)
  • Sartre, Jean-Paul. (1936) 2004. The Transcendence of the Ego: A Sketch for a Phenomenological Description. Translated by Andrew Brown. London: Routledge.
    • Module focus: Part I (the ego and pre-reflective consciousness)
    • Free: None.
    • Publisher: Routledge
    • Free companion: SEP, "Jean-Paul Sartre" (Renaudie)
  • Sartre, Jean-Paul. (1943) 2018. Being and Nothingness: An Essay in Phenomenological Ontology. Translated by Sarah Richmond. London: Routledge.
    • Module focus: Part III, chapters 1–2 (the look; the body)
    • Free: None. Richmond prints the French pagination, so learners using the older Barnes translation can cross-reference.
    • Publisher: Routledge · less expensive US paperback: Washington Square Press
    • Free companion: SEP, "Jean-Paul Sartre" (Renaudie), §4.3
  • Wertheimer, Max. (1923) 1938. "Laws of Organization in Perceptual Forms." Translated by Willis D. Ellis. In A Source Book of Gestalt Psychology, edited by Willis D. Ellis, 71–88. London: Kegan Paul, Trench, Trubner.
    • Module focus: The full essay (short)
    • Free: Classics in the History of Psychology
